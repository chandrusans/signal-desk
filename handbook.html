Chapter 01What this is

Signal Desk is a research tool for a self-directed retail investor doing swing trading in liquid US equities. It scores every constituent of the S&P 500, pulls live prices from Yahoo Finance roughly every 30 seconds, computes a set of real technical indicators, weighs the news flow around each name, and gives every ticker a composite score from 0 to 100.

It also proposes a trade plan — where to enter, where to place your stop, where to take profit — computed from live volatility, not from a hardcoded number. And it tells you when to not trade, because most of the time no clean setup exists.

The single most important idea

The desk is not telling you what to buy. It's screening the tape and producing an opinion. You keep the decision. The tool's job is to make the reasoning transparent so you can disagree with it intelligently.

Chapter 03The composite score

The composite score is a number from 0 to 100 that summarizes everything the desk knows about a stock. Higher is better. It's computed from real market data on each poll, so it changes as the tape moves.

The score is the sum of five base components plus two overlay adjustments:

ComponentWhat it measuresMax
Trend Alignment of price vs moving averages (MA20, MA50, MA200) and the direction of the 20-day MA. Full alignment (price > MA20 > MA50 > MA200) plus upward MA slope = maximum. 30 pts
Momentum
(relative strength)		 Scores outperformance versus SPY, not absolute return. A stock up 5% while SPY is up 9% is a laggard — absolute momentum alone can't see that and systematically buys underperformers in a strong tape. Full marks at +8% or better relative strength over 20 days. The 60-day RS confirms persistence: recent strength with negative 60d RS is a bounce, not a trend, and gets marked down. RSI still applies its overbought/oversold penalty. Falls back to absolute 20d return only when no benchmark is available. 25 pts
Location How close price is to the 52-week high. At/near the high = full points; more than 20% below = zero. 20 pts
Volume Ratio of 5-day average volume to 20-day average volume. Expanding volume (>1.3×) = accumulation, scores high. 15 pts
Volatility 14-day ATR as a % of price. The tradable sweet spot is 1.5–4%; too quiet or too wild scores less. 10 pts
News catalyst Overlay. Live headline sentiment (keyword-scored) from the last 24 hours. More headlines with stronger sentiment moves this more. ±10 pts
Political overlay Overlay. Political headlines are scored using a sector-aware lexicon — "chips act" is bullish for semis but neutral for healthcare, "antitrust" is bearish for big tech, etc. ±8 pts

The base components (trend + momentum + location + volume + volatility) sum to a maximum of 100. The overlays can push the score up or down by an additional 18 combined. The final score is clamped to the 0–100 range.

How to read the score

  • 75+ — every dimension is firing. This is what an A+ setup looks like. Rare.
  • 55–74 — strong trend + momentum, worth watching. May trade if the setup state is BREAKOUT or PULLBACK.
  • 35–54 — mixed. Something is missing (probably trend alignment or volume). Not a trade — a watchlist item.
  • Below 35 — broken or basing. Skip.
Score is not permission to trade

A high score means the tape looks good. It doesn't mean the current bar is a valid entry. Always check the setup state below the score — it's what tells you whether an entry actually exists.

Chapter 04Setup states

Each ticker is classified into one of six mechanical setup states. This tells you what action, if any, the framework thinks is appropriate right now.

BREAKOUT Price is at a 20-day high on expanding volume (5d/20d ratio ≥ 1.2×), RSI is between 55 and 72, and the trend is aligned. This is the cleanest momentum entry — enter at market. Full sizing.

PULLBACK Trend is intact, price has pulled back to within 2% of the 10-day moving average, RSI has cooled to 40–58, and 20-day return is still positive. This is buying the dip inside an uptrend. Enter on close within the pullback zone.

EARLY Uptrend is forming (price above MA20 and MA50) but no confirmed breakout or pullback entry yet. Watchlist only. Don't enter blindly — wait for the setup to graduate to BREAKOUT or PULLBACK.

EXTENDED Price is more than 6% above the MA20 or RSI is above 78. Chasing here has poor risk/reward. Wait for a pullback to MA10/20 before considering entry.

BROKEN Structural trend damage — either price is below the 50-day moving average, or price is below the 20-day and the 20-day has rolled under the 50-day. Skip until the stock builds a new base and reclaims the moving averages on volume.

AVOID Nothing clean. No entry criteria met. Do nothing.

BROKEN vs AVOID — a distinction that matters

Until v3.1, any close below the 20-day MA was labelled BROKEN. Scanning the full S&P 500 exposed how wrong that was: it flagged 208 of 501 names as "trend broken" on a day when 68% of the index was trading above its own 50-day MA. A stock in a healthy uptrend dipping to its 20-day is not a broken trend, and calling it one produced a SELL verdict on names that were fine.

BROKEN now means the structure is actually damaged. A shallow dip that meets no entry criteria falls through to AVOID — "no clean setup on this bar" — which is honest, and no longer triggers a SELL.

EARNINGS Earnings within 5 days. This state overrides every other classification, no matter how good the technicals look. A stock that opens −12% on a miss fills your stop at −12%, not at −1R. You cannot risk-manage an overnight gap — the stop price simply doesn't exist at the open. Wait for the print.

Why earnings gets its own state

A 15-trading-day swing routinely straddles an earnings date. This is the single most common way an ATR-based stop fails in practice: the model assumes you can exit at your stop, and a gap makes that assumption false. Positions already open when earnings approach get an in-window warning so you can exit before the print or deliberately size down and accept the gamble.

Most days, the majority of tickers show EARLY, BROKEN, or AVOID. That's normal — clean setups are rare. The system rejecting a trade is doing its job.

Chapter 05Reading a trade plan

When you open a pick's detail panel, the trade plan block shows four cells:

  • Entry band — the price range to enter within. For BREAKOUT setups it's tight around current price; for PULLBACK it's a zone around the MA10.
  • Stop — where you cut the loss, no exceptions. Calculated as max(1.5 × ATR₁₄, 5%) below entry, capped at 10%.
  • Target — where you take profit. Set at +2.5R above entry, meaning the reward is 2.5× the risk you took.
  • Time stop — 15 trading days. If the trade is flat after three weeks, close it — opportunity cost.

The R-multiple system

All targets and stops are measured in "R" — the initial risk you took. If your entry was $100 and your stop was $95, then R = $5. A +2R gain means the trade is up $10. A +2.5R target means you're aiming for $12.50.

This is the professional way to size trades — instead of fixed dollar profits, everything is expressed relative to your original risk. A 60% win rate at 2.5R average wins is highly profitable even after losses.

The exit checklist

Every detail panel includes a mechanical exit checklist:

  1. Hard stop hit → exit full position, no averaging down.
  2. Price hits +1R → trail stop to breakeven.
  3. Price hits +2R → trail stop to +1R.
  4. Target hit → scale half, trail remainder under MA20.
  5. Flat after 15 trading days → close (opportunity cost).
  6. SPY closes below its 50-day MA → stop opening new positions (this is advice to you; the Dashboard verdicts react to the VIX regime, not to SPY's moving average).
Position sizing

The framework uses risk-parity: risk 1% of capital per trade — the same number the risk engine (Chapter 22) enforces, read from one place. Shares = (capital × 0.01) / stop distance. Wider ATR → smaller position. Every trade risks the same dollar amount regardless of how volatile the stock is.

Chapter 06Regime filter · VIX

The VIX (CBOE Volatility Index) measures expected market volatility. The desk pulls it live and uses it to gate what setups it will trade.

  • VIX < 15 · Calm. Full sizing. All setup types allowed.
  • VIX 15–20 · Normal. Full sizing but only A+ setups (BREAKOUT and PULLBACK).
  • VIX 20–25 · Elevated. Half sizing. PULLBACK entries only — no chasing breakouts into vol.
  • VIX 25–35 · Stress. Quarter sizing at most. No new longs. Manage existing risk.
  • VIX > 35 · Crisis. No new positions. Exit weakest holdings. Hedge the book.

The regime cell in the pulse strip shows the current VIX and what the framework will let you do. The detail panel also shows an explicit "Regime blocks new entry on this setup" line when a candidate's setup isn't allowed under the current regime.

Breadth — the second regime input

VIX alone lags. A calm VIX with collapsing market breadth (2007, January 2020) is precisely when momentum entries stop working: the index is being held up by a handful of mega-caps while the average stock has already rolled over. VIX won't tell you that. Breadth will.

The desk computes breadth as the percentage of the tracked universe trading above its own 50-day moving average — a real, computable measure from data already being fetched, no extra vendor required. It appears in the pulse strip and overrides the VIX-derived regime:

  • Below 30% — regime forced to DIVERGENT. No new longs regardless of VIX. The tape is broken underneath a calm surface.
  • 30–45% — sizing halved, pullbacks only. Narrow tape.
  • Above 65% — breadth confirms the regime; normal sizing applies.

When breadth and VIX disagree, breadth wins. The more conservative of the two signals always governs.

Chapter 07Political overlay

Political events move stocks materially. Tariffs, sanctions, drug-pricing bills, antitrust rulings, FOMC decisions — these can matter more than any technical setup for weeks at a time.

Every headline in the news feed is scanned for political trigger words (tariff, antitrust, fomc, sanctions, chips act, congress, etc.). If any match, the headline gets a POL badge in the intel feed.

Sector-aware sentiment

Political sentiment is not a global bull/bear score — the same word can mean opposite things depending on the sector. Each pinned ticker is tagged with a political sector:

  • semis — NVDA, AMD, ASML, MRVL. "Chips act" bullish, "export controls" bearish.
  • defense — PLTR, CAT. "Budget increase" and "conflict" bullish, "peace talks" bearish.
  • energy — XOM. "Drilling permit" bullish, "climate rules" bearish.
  • healthcare — LLY, NVAX. "FDA approval" bullish, "drug pricing" bearish.
  • tech-mega — META, MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, CRWD. "Antitrust" bearish, "settled" bullish.
  • crypto — HOOD, COIN. "Spot ETF approved" bullish, "SEC crackdown" bearish.
  • ev — TSLA, F. "Tax credit" bullish, "credit removed" bearish.

The political overlay adjusts the composite score by up to ±8 points based on the volume and average sentiment of political headlines in the last 24 hours, weighted through the ticker's sector lexicon.

Chapter 08The screener

The candidate list scans the entire S&P 500

The ranked candidate list at the top of the Stocks tab is not a hand-picked watchlist. Every name in the S&P 500 is scored on every scan — roughly 500 tickers, each with a year of daily bars, relative strength against SPY and against its own GICS sector ETF. The constituent list is pulled live, so index additions and removals follow automatically.

Use the Sector filter to narrow to one GICS sector, Setup and Verdict to narrow to what's actionable, and Show to control how many rows render. The status line reports how many names were scored; ↻ Rescan forces a fresh pass.

Why scoring happens on the server

500 tickers × a year of daily bars is about 13 MB of raw data — too large to send to a browser, and hopeless on mobile. So the scoring runs server-side and each ticker comes back as a compact ~400-byte row: score, setup, the indicators the card needs, and a 20-point sparkline. The whole index arrives in roughly 195 KB. Full bar history is fetched for exactly one ticker — the one you click.

A side benefit: everyone sees identical, reproducible scores, instead of each browser computing its own.

Because the composite caps at 100 and a dozen or more names can tie there across 500 tickers, ties are broken by sector-relative strength first, then relative strength vs SPY. Beating SPY while lagging your own sector is not leadership.

The lower screener table

Below the fold, the screener runs the same composite scoring against a smaller fixed universe with additional fundamental columns. Use it when you want market cap and P/E alongside the technical score.

Filter controls:

  • Sector — GICS-style sector filter (Semiconductors, Software, Cybersecurity, etc.)
  • Min composite score — drop everything below your threshold
  • Min market cap — live, from NASDAQ via /api/fundamentals
  • Max P/E — currently static
  • Min momentum (20d) — live, computed from real bars
  • Min sentiment — currently static

Every column header is clickable to sort. Setup states are shown as chips in the Setup column.

Chapter 09Political calendar

A curated list of upcoming events that historically move markets. Auto-hides past events and sorts by nearest first. Each event shows:

  • Days until — countdown in the leftmost slot; TODAY when it hits.
  • Tag — FOMC / ELECTION / CHINA / FISCAL / GOVSHUT / MACRO.

Chapter 10Data sources

Live

  • Prices, OHLC, volume, VIX — Yahoo Finance chart API. No auth key. Polled every 15s (Stocks) / 20s (Crypto), behind a 30s edge cache — see Chapter 17 for the full cadence table.
  • News headlines — Yahoo Finance search endpoint. Also no key. Pulled on each poll.
  • Sentiment (general + political) — computed client-side from headlines using keyword lexicons.
  • All technical indicators — computed from the 1-year OHLC series (server-side for the index scan, in the browser for the ticker you open).
  • S&P 500 constituents and GICS sectors — /api/universe, sourced from the datasets/s-and-p-500-companies list that tracks the index table. Cached 24 hours; index changes follow automatically. Falls back to an embedded mega-cap list if the source is unreachable, so the app degrades to "fewer tickers" rather than "broken".
  • Index-wide composite scores — /api/scan, scored server-side in batches of 50. Cached 60 seconds at the edge.
  • Sector-relative strength — each batch fetches the SPDR sector ETFs it needs (XLK, XLV, XLF, XLY, XLC, XLI, XLP, XLE, XLU, XLRE, XLB) over the same window as the tickers being scored.
  • Market breadth — percentage of the full S&P 500 above its own 50-day MA, computed from the scan rather than from a handful of polled names.
  • Economic calendar — /api/intel?op=macro, from the FairEconomy JSON feed that powers ForexFactory's own calendar widget. Drives the macro event gate.
  • Congressional trades — /api/intel?op=congress, parsed from Periodic Transaction Reports published by the Clerk of the House (the primary STOCK Act filings behind sites like CapitolTrades). Context badge only — deliberately not scored, see Chapter 16.

Still mock or static

  • Market cap and P/E in the screener table — live from NASDAQ (/api/fundamentals); P/E is GAAP trailing.
  • Insider / options-flow narratives in the older intel section — placeholder data.
  • Political calendar — curated list, not scraped. Update by editing the array in index.html.
  • Reddit mention counts — blocked without OAuth (see README for setup).

Chapter 11Glossary

  • ATR — Average True Range. A measure of a stock's typical daily price movement in dollars. Used to size stops so they respect the stock's natural volatility.
  • RSI — Relative Strength Index. Momentum oscillator from 0 to 100. Above 70 = overbought, below 30 = oversold. Between 40 and 70 is where healthy trends live.
  • SMA — Simple Moving Average. Average closing price over N days. Trend-following indicator.
  • MA alignment — When shorter MAs sit above longer ones (price > MA20 > MA50 > MA200). Confirms an uptrend across timeframes.
  • R-multiple — Trade result expressed as a multiple of initial risk. A +2R winner made twice what you were willing to lose.
  • R:R ratio — Reward-to-risk ratio. A 3:1 setup risks $1 to make $3.
  • Regime — The current volatility environment, quantified by VIX. Dictates position sizing and which setups are eligible.
  • Composite score — The 0–100 rank Signal Desk assigns each ticker, summing across trend / momentum / location / volume / volatility / catalysts / political.
  • Setup state — A mechanical classification of what action, if any, is appropriate: BREAKOUT, PULLBACK, EARLY, EXTENDED, BROKEN, AVOID.
  • FOMC — Federal Open Market Committee. The Fed body that sets interest rates. Meetings are the biggest scheduled market-moving events.
  • Section 301 — US trade-law tool for imposing tariffs. Reviews typically move semis, EV, and solar stocks.

Chapter 12Dashboard · your positions

The Dashboard is your personal portfolio view. Anything you actually own goes here; the desk then tracks it live and — most importantly — tells you when a seasoned PM would sell it. This isn't a P&L calculator. It's a decision-support layer for every open position.

Adding a position

Fill the Add form with ticker, share count, buy price, buy date, and optionally a manual stop. Everything is stored locally in your browser (no server, no account). Use Export JSON regularly if you care about the data — clear browser data and it's gone.

If you don't set a stop, the desk defaults to 5% below entry — a reasonable equity swing default. Override it any time via the Edit button.

The verdict system

Every position gets one of four verdicts, refreshed on each poll (15s). Verdicts are colour-coded on the card's left rail and sorted so the ones needing attention float to the top:

  • SELL Exit the whole position. Reasons that trigger it: hard stop hit, setup BROKEN while the trade is below +0.5R (a fresh one still above −0.5R gets WATCH instead), regime turned CRISIS (VIX ≥ 35) — or STRESS (25–35) while the position is losing, time stop with no follow-through after 15 days. In STRESS a winner gets WATCH: move the stop to breakeven and let it decide. Metals are exempt from the regime rule; gold is the hedge that stress is supposed to pay for.
  • TRIM Take partial profit. Reasons: RSI overbought (>78) on a >2R winner, setup went EXTENDED on a winner, MA20 slope negative on a winner.
  • HOLD Do nothing yet. Fresh positions, or profitable positions with trend intact. The reason line will tell you where to trail your stop.
  • WATCH Decision imminent. Price is within a fraction of R of your stop or target — be at your screen.

How the sell logic thinks

The verdict function checks rules in priority order and returns the first match:

  1. Hard stop hit — no exceptions, no averaging down.
  2. Regime turned — CRISIS (VIX ≥ 35) sells the book; STRESS (25–35) sells losers and moves winners' stops to breakeven.
  3. Setup broken with loss — trend dead, cut before it compounds.
  4. Time stop — 15 trading days with less than 1R gain means the thesis didn't materialize. Free the capital for a better setup.
  5. Overextended winner — RSI > 78 with a >2R gain, or setup gone EXTENDED. Scale half, move stop up.
  6. Trend deteriorating on a winner — MA20 slope negative with >1R gain. Protect gains before they roundtrip.
  7. Fresh position — first 3 days, always HOLD to give the setup room.
  8. Winner with intact trend — HOLD, trail stop per R-multiple gained.
  9. Near stop or target — WATCH.
Personalization

You can override any position's stop via the Edit button. The verdict engine will re-evaluate against your custom stop on the next poll. Notes fields (planned) let you tag entries with your thesis so future-you knows why past-you bought it.

Portfolio summary strip

Above the position list: portfolio value, unrealized P&L in $ and %, position count, count of action alerts (sell + trim), and current regime. When SELL or TRIM alerts > 0, the alert cell turns red — a nudge to open the dashboard.

Import / Export / Clear

Export JSON downloads your full portfolio as a plain JSON file — commit it to a private repo if you want history. Import reads a previously-exported file and replaces the current portfolio (with confirmation). Clear wipes everything after confirmation. These give you data portability without any server dependency.

Data lives only in your browser

Positions are stored in localStorage under key sd-portfolio-v1. Clearing site data, switching browsers, or using incognito will make them disappear. Export before doing any of that.

Chapter 13Options · Robinhood & Schwab

The Options tab surfaces up to 10 single-leg call or put contracts ranked for the two brokers you actually use: Robinhood and Charles Schwab. Contract details, entry, exits, stop, breakeven, and max loss are all pre-computed. The scored contracts are the ones a discretionary trader would spend time inspecting.

Data source

NASDAQ's public options-chain JSON endpoint (no API key). Refreshed on button click. For each ticker, ~2000 raw contracts get scored, filtered, and the single best short-term and long-term contract per direction is returned.

Scoring model

Each contract is scored from 0–100 across five factors. Contracts that fail any hard filter (e.g. under 50 open interest, spread wider than 20%) are excluded entirely — not shown at any score:

  • DTE fit (30 pts) — short-term sweet spot is 21–35 days to expiration; long-term is 120–270. Farther from the sweet spot loses points.
  • Moneyness (25 pts) — slightly OTM (strike/spot 1.00–1.10 for calls, inverse for puts) is the sweet spot for R-multiple efficiency.
  • Open Interest (20 pts) — ≥1000 is a clean market, ≥200 is fine, under 50 is filtered out.
  • Spread (15 pts) — bid-ask under 5% is tight; over 20% is filtered out (you'll get sliced on the fill).
  • Volume today (10 pts) — trailing activity confirms real liquidity right now.

What each card shows

  • Direction chip — CALL (green) or PUT (red) with a matching left rail.
  • Contract line — strike, expiry date, DTE, short-term vs long-term tag.
  • Bid / ask / mid — the mid is your limit-order price. Do not use market orders on options.
  • Per-contract premium — mid × 100. This is what you pay for one contract.
  • Estimated delta — from moneyness (not Black-Scholes). Ballpark for choosing between contracts.
  • Liquidity rating — good / okay / thin. Prefer good.
  • Copy OCC symbol — one click copies e.g. NVDA260814C00215000. Paste that into Robinhood's or Schwab's search bar and the contract opens directly, no clicking through the chain.
  • + Track — writes the contract to the Dashboard as an option position with strike / expiry / direction / multiplier=100 so it shows up next to your stock positions with P&L on premium.

The analytics strip · six cells you actually trade off

  • Entry (limit) — the mid price. Submit as a limit order.
  • Stop (−50%) — exit if premium halves. This is the mechanical stop for long options; most books use −50% because premium can decay fast.
  • Target +50% — premium goes to 1.5× entry. Underlying price required is estimated using delta.
  • Target +100% — premium doubles. Same estimation caveat.
  • Breakeven at expiry — the underlying price where you break even if held to expiration. For calls: strike + premium. For puts: strike − premium.
  • Max loss — premium per contract + $0.65 regulatory fee (both Robinhood and Schwab). This is the whole ballgame if you're wrong.

Broker realities

  • Both apps charge $0.65/contract as a regulatory pass-through. Commission itself is $0 on Robinhood and $0 base on Schwab.
  • Both accept the OCC symbol format used on every card.
  • Both are American-style — assignment risk if in-the-money at expiry. This framework's positions are long-only single-leg, so assignment isn't a concern unless you're the seller (not our recommendation).
Long options can lose 100%

Unlike stocks, a long option can decay to zero even if you're eventually right — theta and time-to-expiry are unforgiving. Never size a position where the max loss is more than you're prepared to lose. Delta on these cards is an estimate; verify greeks in your broker before entering.

Chapter 14Metals page · XAU/USD & XAG/USD

The metals page is a dedicated view for gold and silver. Metals trade on different drivers than equities — Fed rates, dollar strength, real yields, geopolitical risk — so they get their own dashboard rather than sitting inside the equity screener.

Live pricing source

Yahoo Finance does not serve XAUUSD=X or XAGUSD=X directly. The page uses GC=F (COMEX Gold continuous futures) and SI=F (Silver futures) as the live proxy — basis vs spot is under 0.5%, so the projected ranges hold at spot too. Both symbols are displayed to you as XAU/USD and XAG/USD per market convention.

Range projection · the math

For each metal, two ranges are projected off the current price and 14-day ATR:

  • Day range = price ± 0.5 × ATR₁₄. This is a ~1σ band for a normal trading day — about 68% of days close inside it.
  • Week range = price ± ATR₁₄ × √5. Variance scales with the square root of time; a week is roughly 2.24× the daily band, not 5×. A common beginner error is to multiply by 5.

A Fed decision, CPI print, or geopolitical shock can and does widen the actual weekly move well beyond this projection. The band is a base case, not a promise.

Directional bias

Each panel labels the tape as one of four states:

  • BULL BIAS — full MA alignment + MA20 sloping up. Buy pullbacks to MA10/20.
  • CONSTRUCTIVE — above MA20 and MA50 but 200d not confirmed. With-trend only.
  • BEAR BIAS — below MA20 with negative slope. Fade rallies; longs need MA20 reclaim.
  • RANGE — no clear trend. Sell resistance, buy support.

Drivers strip

The top strip shows the four things you need in your peripheral vision when trading metals:

  • DXY — US dollar index. Dollar down = metals up. Inversely coloured (a DXY drop is green because it's a metals tailwind).
  • US 10Y — Treasury yield. Rising yields = headwind for gold (opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset). Inversely coloured.
  • VIX — risk regime. High VIX often supports gold's safe-haven bid. Silver is more industrial and can suffer with growth fears.
  • Gold-Silver ratio — XAU / XAG. Above 85 historically means silver is cheap vs gold. Below 60 means silver rich. Traders use extremes for pair trades.

Metals calendar

The events that move metals most: FOMC, CPI, NFP, Jackson Hole, US elections. Countdown shows days-until; impact colour flags priority. High-impact events within 5 days = reduce size regardless of technicals.

Trade plan

Same framework as equities but tuned for metals volatility: stop = max(1.5 × ATR, 2%), target = +2.5R, time stop = 15 days. Metals have lower percentage volatility than stocks so the 2% floor is tighter than the equity 5%.

Not all metals move together

Gold trades on rates + geopolitics. Silver has ~50% industrial demand, so it also cares about global manufacturing PMIs and copper's direction. When gold rips on a Fed cut and silver lags, that's typically a signal to fade silver's underperformance via the gold-silver ratio.

Chapter 15Crypto · and why fees decide it

The Crypto tab scores the top coins by market cap that are actually tradeable as USD pairs on Coinbase. Ranking a coin you cannot buy is noise, so the universe is the intersection of the two lists, not just the market-cap leaderboard.

Read this before using the rankings

Backtested over roughly five years of daily candles, the momentum strategy has a small positive edge before costs and a negative edge after real Coinbase fees. The exchange takes the entire edge and more. The page shows this measured result at the top and does not hide it behind the rankings — because a screen that looks authoritative while losing money is worse than no screen at all.

What changes for crypto

  • The benchmark is BTC, not SPY. Relative strength is measured against Bitcoin. An alt up 6% while BTC is up 10% is a laggard, and in a BTC drawdown it will fall harder.
  • Leadership needs a higher bar. Beating the benchmark by 8% over 20 days earns full momentum marks for a stock; for a coin the threshold is 15%, because that much dispersion is ordinary noise in crypto.
  • Wider stop bounds. Crypto ATR runs 2–3× equities — BTC around 2.3% a day, small alts near 7%. The stop is max(1.5 × ATR, 3%) capped at 15%, against 5%/10% for equities. Equity bounds would clip high-volatility coins to a flat 10% and destroy the ATR-proportionality that makes R-multiples comparable.
  • A tighter floor. Crypto trades 24/7, so there is no overnight gap to buffer against and the floor can sit below the equity 5%.
  • No earnings gate. There is no crypto equivalent, so that override is switched off.

The regime filter without a VIX

There is no volatility index for crypto, so the regime input is BTC's own realised volatility — the annualised standard deviation of its daily log returns over 30 days, annualised by 365 rather than 252 because crypto trades every day.

  • Under 35% — CALM, full sizing
  • 35–55% — NORMAL, A+ setups only
  • 55–75% — ELEVATED, half size, pullbacks only
  • 75–110% — STRESS, no new longs
  • Above 110% — CRISIS, risk-off

Two overrides sit on top. BTC below its own 50-day MA with a calm volatility reading forces DIVERGENT — a quiet downtrend is distribution, and alts fall harder than BTC in that tape. Universe breadth below 30% does the same.

"What time should I sell?"

Crypto has no opening bell, so timing has no calendar answer — but it might have a measurable one, since Asia, Europe and US sessions overlap at different UTC hours. Open any coin and the page shows the average return for each of the 24 hours, from about 100 days of hourly candles.

Why each hour carries a t-statistic

Testing 24 hours and reporting the best one will always find a "winner", even in pure noise — that is multiple-comparison bias, and it is how most published trading-time rules are manufactured. So every hour is t-tested against zero and judged against a Bonferroni-corrected threshold (alpha 0.05 ÷ 24 tests, |t| ≥ 3.09). Bars outlined in gold cleared it.

On the majors tested so far, none do. The honest reading is that time of day is not a reliable edge, and the page says so rather than naming a spurious best hour.

Why a fixed daily profit target does not work

The page carries an explicit answer to "how do I make $50 a day", because it is the most common way people lose money in this market. Two independent problems:

  • The measured edge is negative after fees. Trading a negative edge more often, or with more capital, loses faster. No position size fixes it, so the page refuses to print a capital figure.
  • Even a profitable system does not pay daily. This one produces well under one signal a day across the whole universe, and around 70% of the trades it does take are losers. Profit, when it exists, comes from a few large winners — not a steady drip.

For scale: at an edge of +0.20R — better than anything measured here and better than most professional systematic strategies sustain — $50/day at this signal rate would still need roughly six figures of capital at 1% risk per trade. The daily-income framing needs serious money even when the strategy works.

The fee table

The reality panel sweeps the same backtest across venue fee tiers, from Coinbase retail down to zero. It exists because venue choice, not coin choice, is the biggest single lever on whether this strategy has an edge at all. Breakeven sits far below Coinbase's retail taker fee. Read the table before assuming a better coin list would fix the result.

Chapter 16Macro calendar & congressional trades

The macro event gate

The pulse strip carries a Macro cell fed by ForexFactory's economic calendar. It is a risk gate, not a forecast — it never predicts which way a release lands, only whether one is close enough to gap the tape through your stop.

  • High-impact US release within 24 hours → BLOCKED. Every verdict becomes AVOID. An ATR stop is priced off normal daily range and does not trade at that level through a gap.
  • Within 48 hours → CAUTION, half size on new entries.
  • Otherwise CLEAR, with the next major release named.

The releases that trigger it are the ones that move the whole index — NFP, CPI, PPI, PCE, FOMC, ISM, retail sales, GDP, jobless claims, and Fed chair appearances. This gate applies to Crypto too: BTC reacts to a CPI print about as hard as the S&P does.

Why the feed and not the website

forexfactory.com/calendar returns 403 to anything that is not a browser. Rather than work around a bot-protection control, the desk reads the FairEconomy JSON feed — the publisher's own machine-readable endpoint for exactly this data.

Congressional trades — shown, not scored

Cards can carry a CONGRESS badge showing net buys minus sells and how many members traded that name. The data comes from Periodic Transaction Reports filed under the STOCK Act and published by the Clerk of the House — the same primary filings that sites like CapitolTrades present.

It is context because it was tested and failed

Across 2,379 disclosed 2026 stock transactions, excess return versus SPY measured from the notification date — the first moment the public could act:

  • BUY +5d  −0.13% (t=−0.71)  ·  BUY +20d  −0.49% (t=−1.39), only 45% beat SPY
  • SELL +5d −0.28% (t=−1.72)  ·  SELL +20d −0.34% (t=−0.99)

Nothing is statistically significant, and following disclosed buys slightly under-performed the index. The cause is structural: the median disclosure lag is 18 days and the 90th percentile is 33. By the time a trade is visible it is roughly a month old.

So it earns a badge and nothing more. Feeding it into the composite score would be adding noise with a compelling story attached.

Coverage is partial by nature: roughly 60% of PTRs yield machine-readable transactions, the rest being scans or amendments. The endpoint reports its own coverage so you can see what fraction was parsed.

pump.fun — an exit-risk screen, not a pick list

The Crypto tab carries a pump.fun section sourced from DexScreener's public API, which indexes pumpswap (pump.fun's own DEX). pump.fun's endpoints return 403 behind Cloudflare and are not used.

What the live sample actually shows

Across a live sample of roughly 220 pump.fun pairs:

  • Only about 22% have enough depth and activity to exit a small position (liquidity ≥ $10k and 24h volume ≥ $1k).
  • Around 37% report no liquidity at all.
  • Median 24h volume collapses from roughly $4,500 on day one to under $60 after a week — a ~99% decay.
  • About 20% have a pool worth under 5% of market cap, so the quoted valuation cannot be realised.

Two biases make the truth worse. Only tokens that completed the bonding curve and reached a DEX are indexed at all — everything that died before graduating is invisible. And DexScreener search ranks by activity, so the sample over-represents busy tokens. A narrower query set reported 73% "exitable" where the wider one reports 22%; that gap is sampling bias, not the market.

Because of this the section scores exit risk, sorted so the tokens you are most likely to be able to sell appear first. High is bad. It does not rank tokens to buy, and no measurement on this desk predicts which memecoin rises.

The rulebook it supports is about sizing and exit, not entry:

  1. Size to the pool. The Max position column is 1% of pool liquidity — above that your own sale moves the price against you.
  2. Assume total loss. Size so that a position going to zero is survivable. On this venue that is the correct default, not pessimism.
  3. Check the exit first. Under $1k of 24h volume there is no market to sell into.
  4. Time-stop in hours. Volume decays ~99% within a week; there is no long-term hold here because the market disappears underneath the position.
  5. Ignore paid boosts. They are advertising slots bought by promoters. The table flags them so they are not mistaken for demand.

Chapter 17How to read the desk

If the rest of the desk feels like too much, use the Today tab

The Today tab is the whole rulebook reduced to three questions, answered in plain sentences: Should I trade today? What qualifies across stocks, options, crypto and metals? What do I leave alone? It reads the same live data and applies the same rules as every other tab — it just puts the answer first and the evidence second.

Enter your trading capital once and every candidate is sized for you: how many shares or contracts, and the most you can lose if the stop is hit. Each card says in one sentence why it qualifies and exactly when to sell. When nothing qualifies, it says so — that is the normal state, not a fault.

Everything else in this handbook exists so you can check the Today tab's work. You do not need to read it to use the tab.

The desk now pulls from a dozen sources. They are not equal, and treating them as if they were is the main way to misuse it. Everything on the site falls into one of three tiers.

Tier 1 — Gates. These decide whether you trade at all.

  • Macro cell — a high-impact US release inside 24h forces every verdict to AVOID; inside 48h it halves sizing.
  • Regime cell — VIX plus breadth for stocks, BTC realised volatility for crypto. STRESS, CRISIS or DIVERGENT means no new longs.

If a gate is red, stop there. Nothing in the lower tiers overrides it.

Tier 2 — Signal. Only this ranks anything.

  • Composite score, setup state and verdict. BREAKOUT and PULLBACK at a good score are the only genuine entries. EXTENDED means "strong, but wait for a pullback" — it is a HOLD, not a buy.
  • The trade plan — entry band, stop, target, size. Read it before deciding, not after.

Tier 3 — Context. Looks like signal. Is not. Never scored.

  • Congressional badges — measured at −0.49% vs SPY at +20 days, median 18-day disclosure lag (Chapter 16).
  • pump.fun table — sorted by exit risk, not upside. A low score means "you could probably sell it", not "it will rise".
  • Trader buzz and paid boosts — attention, not demand.
The decision order
  1. Gates first. Macro red or regime stressed? That is the answer for today.
  2. Filter to BUY. Typically a couple of dozen names out of 500. Most days the honest answer is "nothing qualifies".
  3. Read the plan before committing. If you are not willing to take the stop, do not take the entry.
  4. Size from the plan, not from conviction — 1% risk × the regime multiplier. On pump.fun, 1% of pool liquidity, hard cap.
  5. Exit mechanically. Stop hit → out. Target → scale. Flat after 15 days → out. This is the part people abandon, and it is where the framework earns its keep.

Chapter 18Simulate — paper auto-trading

The Simulate tab lets the rulebook trade pretend money automatically over a window you choose, and shows every decision it made — including what it is still holding right now. No real order is ever placed. It is the auto-trader experience with none of the auto-trader risk.

What you set

  • Investment — the starting capital.
  • Start trading on — the rulebook begins deciding from this date and runs to today. The engine needs 200 bars of history before its first decision, so a start earlier than the data allows is moved forward and the page says so.
  • Max days to hold each trade — the time-stop. A trade that is flat after this many days is sold.
  • Market — 30 liquid stocks, 12 major coins, or both (capital split evenly).
  • Strategy — the rulebook (breakout / pullback), mean-reversion on RSI-2 dips, or the composite score.
  • Risk per trade, max open positions, crypto fee — the same knobs the live rules use. Stocks are charged 7 bps a side; crypto defaults to Coinbase's 60.

Presets fill these in — Rulebook, Conservative, Aggressive, Crypto on Coinbase, Crypto on a low-fee venue, Stocks + crypto — so you can compare like for like with one click.

What you get back

  • Start → end equity, with the same-window buy-and-hold return of SPY or BTC beside it. A strategy that made 4% while the index made 12% did not win.
  • Realised P&L, what is still open (marked at the last close), win rate, expectancy after fees, worst drawdown, and how many signals were skipped because every slot was full.
  • An equity curve, the open positions with their live stop and target, and every closed trade with entry, exit, why it sold and dollar P&L.
How it is built — and why there is no server

It is layered the way auto-invest tools are: a strategy layer (the rulebook in analyze.js), an execution engine (backtest.js — point-in-time decisions, fills at the next bar's open, fees and slippage, gap-aware stops, a position cap), and an accounting layer in the page (cash, equity path, dollar P&L, open positions marked to market).

The scheduler is the interesting part. A paper portfolio is a pure function of its configuration and market history, so it does not need a cron job or a database: every visit recomputes it from the bars. Saved simulations store only their settings and are re-run live each time you open the tab — no drift, no stale state, nothing to sync. They live on this device only.

Read a good result carefully

This is honest but it is still history. Fills, fees and gaps are modelled; your own hesitation, a wider spread on the day, or a halt are not. Ninety days is a short window and can flatter any strategy. Run the same settings from several start dates before believing a number — and on crypto, remember the measured edge at retail fees is negative over the long run regardless of what one window shows.

Chapter 22Risk engine & trade journal — the book has the final say

The exit rulebook (Chapter 20) decides when one position is sold. The risk engine decides whether the whole book can take another position at all. Both the Today tab and the Dashboard read it; Today sizes every pick to what is left of the budget, and when the budget is gone it shows no picks — by design, the same way a closed macro gate does.

The five limits

The reason is always printed next to the stamp. Track still works when blocked, after a confirmation that says you are acting against the rules — the desk does not stop you, it makes you say so.

The limits live in one place, risk.js, and are shared by both tabs. They are the values most systematic desks run; the handbook states them so you can disagree with them knowingly.

The journal — your own expectancy, not the backtest's

Press Close on a Dashboard position and enter the price you sold at. The trade lands in the journal with its R-multiple: profit divided by the risk you took at entry (entry minus the initial stop). The initial stop is frozen the first time you move a stop, so trailing to breakeven — which the rulebook tells you to do — never changes what 1R means, the 2.5R target, or the time stop. The active stop still decides when you are out. +2R means you made twice what you were prepared to lose; −1R is a clean stop-out. The journal then shows what the Simulate tab shows for the backtest, measured on you:

  • Win rate, average win and average loss in R, and expectancy — average R per trade. Positive and you are being paid to follow the rules; negative and either the rules or the following is broken.
  • Profit factor (gross wins ÷ gross losses) and realized P&L in dollars.
  • Breakdown by asset class and by the exit rule that was showing when you closed — so you can see whether your stop-outs, targets and time stops behave like the backtest's.
  • A confidence note: with fewer than ~30 trades the expectancy is not distinguishable from zero. The rulebook backtests at about +0.07R on stocks; it takes dozens of trades to see that in your own numbers. Do not change the rules on ten trades.

Trades you made outside the app can be added by hand (+ Add a past trade). Export CSV or JSON any time — the journal is stored only in this browser, and is not sent to the phone-alert server.

Why "closed today" uses realized losses only, on New York time

Open losses are already counted in the heat limit. Counting them twice would close the desk every time a position wobbled. The daily limit is for losses you have taken; the heat limit is for losses you could take.

The forensic checks

  • Cash flow vs profit. Operating cash flow divided by net income, every year; years profit rose while cash fell; years cash was negative while profit was positive; accruals as % of assets. Verdict REAL (≥0.9× on average, no bad years, gap not widening), WATCH, or MANUFACTURED? (<0.7×, or negative-cash years, or repeated divergence with a widening gap). The research behind it is Sloan (1996): high-accrual firms underperform.
  • Working capital. Debtor, inventory and creditor days and the cash conversion cycle, five years. MORE EFFICIENT if the cycle shortened 5+ days, CONSUMING CASH if it lengthened 10+ days or customers take 10+ more days to pay.
  • Moat, 0–10. Gross margin level (3), margin trend (2), ROCE (3), and whether margins held through the 2022–23 inflation cycle (2). WIDE ≥8, NARROW ≥5. The kind of moat is a judgment the page leaves to you.
  • Capital allocation. Five-year totals of free cash flow, dividends, buybacks, capex, R&D, acquisitions and stock compensation; share-count change; debt change. VALUE CREATORS needs a shrinking share count or returns funded from operations, ROIC ≥15%, and no acquisition binge that diluted returns; VALUE DESTROYERS is the reverse.
  • Executive pay as % of net income (SEC pay-versus-performance tag) — the US stand-in for "promoter salary"; promoter pledging has no US equivalent, and related-party loans are 10-K prose.
  • Multibagger criteria, honestly adapted: revenue compounding ≥10%, ROIC ≥15% and holding, core reinvestment vs acquisitions. "Fewer than 5 analysts" fails for every S&P 500 name by construction, and insider ownership is not in free structured data — both are marked as such instead of scored.

Conventions, so an analyst can check the arithmetic

  • ROCE = EBIT ÷ average (total assets − current liabilities). ROIC = EBIT × (1 − effective tax rate) ÷ average (equity + debt − cash and marketable securities), undefined when book equity has been run down by buybacks below 10% of assets. Accruals = (net income − operating cash flow) ÷ average assets.
  • Cash-flow quality uses ΣCFO ÷ Σnet income over the window (not a mean of yearly ratios), needs three profitable years, and only treats profit above cash as a concern — cash above profit is depreciation and stock compensation, not a flag.
  • FCF = CFO − capex (property, plant and equipment, plus capitalised software and intangibles where tagged). EV = market cap + debt − all marketable securities, current and noncurrent; leases excluded. Filers without an operating-income line get EBIT = pre-tax income + interest.
  • Multiples are priced at the fiscal-year-end month close against that year's GAAP figures; years above 100× are excluded from medians, and a median needs three years. Fair-value anchors use TTM GAAP EPS, and 3-year average FCF and EBITDA to smooth one-offs. For financials only P/E is used.
  • Payables, receivables, inventory, debt and equity never mix tag definitions across years; where the only tag is "payables and accrued liabilities", creditor days are overstated and the page says so. Splits are taken from the company's filed split ratio, with a size-jump heuristic as fallback.
  • Credibility: a beat is a beat, but an average surprise above 15% is guidance management and caps the score at 7.

Three things to know

  • Banks and insurers get N/A on cash flow, working capital, moat and allocation — those measures are meaningless for a balance-sheet business — and a question about credit quality instead.
  • Splits. Older filings report pre-split share counts and EPS; the page detects a ≥1.8× jump between adjacent years and scales the older years, and says so under the valuation table.
  • Fair value is not a forecast. It is the stock's own median multiples applied to its latest numbers, ±10%. A great business above its range is still a great business; the range tells you what you are paying for that.

Every section ends in a copy-prompt button: the thirteen research prompts (the seven above plus the forensic ones) pre-filled with the filed numbers, so the qualitative work — transcripts, 10-K prose, the kind of moat — can be done in Claude with the right figures in front of it instead of from memory.

Chapter 24Research deep-dives — the "institutional prompt" set, done with data

The popular "act as a Goldman / Morgan Stanley / Bridgewater analyst" prompts ask for ten things each. Most of them are arithmetic on public data, so the Research tab now does that part itself and hands only the narrative part (SWOT, guidance language, Fed outlook) to a pre-filled prompt. Every section says where its numbers come from and what it refused to invent.

Technical report card (display only)

Daily, weekly and monthly trend from the 50/100/200-day, 10/40-week and 6/12-month averages; RSI 14; MACD 12-26-9 with its cross; Bollinger %B and bandwidth; 20-session support and resistance; volume ratio; the rulebook's plan and R:R. It is labelled display-only because none of MACD, Bollinger or chart patterns added to the backtest — only the setup, score and ATR plan drive verdicts. Chart-pattern names and Fibonacci levels are omitted on purpose.

Pre-earnings brief

From NASDAQ: the next report date (flagged as an estimate until confirmed), consensus EPS for the coming quarters with the estimate count and four-week revisions, and the last reports' EPS vs consensus. From price history: the gap at the open, the close-to-close move and the five-session drift after each of those reports. From the option chain: the at-the-money straddle for the first expiry on or after the report, as % of price — the move the market is paying for — compared with the realised average. From the short-interest table: shares short, days to cover and the change since the prior settlement. The decision summary is the desk's rule: no new entry inside five days, holders with +1R trim before the print. Segment detail and guidance language need the transcript and are left to the prompt.

Forward DCF

Five years of revenue (growth fading linearly from the trailing CAGR, capped at 25%, to the terminal rate) and operating margin (3-year average), NOPAT, D&A and capex as % of revenue, 2% of new revenue tied up in working capital, free cash flow discounted at a WACC built from the 10-year yield, the stock's beta and a 5% equity-risk premium, plus the after-tax cost of debt weighted by market cap and debt. Terminal value both ways: perpetuity growth and an exit multiple (the stock's own median EV/EBITDA, capped at 25×), each shown with what it implies about the other. A sensitivity grid across WACC ±2 pts and terminal growth ±1 pt, and a list of which assumptions swing the answer most. Every box is editable; the verdict needs a 20% margin either way. It is a calculator: the moment you change a box the answer is yours.

Patterns

Seasonality by calendar month and day-of-week on up to ten years of daily bars, each with a t-statistic and Bonferroni correction (twelve and five tests respectively), so a month must clear p < 0.004 to be marked. Earnings behaviour (up-days, average gap, five-day drift), short interest and squeeze potential, options premium vs realised, insider filings in the last 90 days, and the statistical-edge summary. Institutional 13F trends and Fed-day correlations are named as not available rather than approximated.

Macro from official data

FRED series (BLS, BEA, Treasury): CPI year-over-year and three-month annualised with its trend, unemployment with the Sahm-rule check, three-month payroll average, real GDP last quarter and year-over-year, fed funds level and direction, the 10-year yield and the 10y−2y curve. A rule-based regime read — expansion, reflation, slowdown, stagflation-risk — with the textbook sector tendencies for it. Fed forecasts and geopolitics are not in any feed and are left to the prompt.

Compare

Two to seven tickers side by side from their own filings: market cap, revenue and its growth, gross and operating margin, ROIC, R&D intensity, free cash flow, net debt/EBITDA, P/E, EV/EBITDA, FCF yield, moat score, credibility, the profits / allocation / valuation verdicts and the five-year share-count change. A rank-sum across the quality and value metrics names the best business by the numbers, with the explicit caution that it is not the same as the best stock. "Compare with the biggest names in its sector" picks the five largest peers by market cap.

Dividend blueprint

A fixed universe of 84 long-record payers. Yield is cash actually paid in the last twelve months (Yahoo dividend events) divided by price, not a quote field. Payout from trailing GAAP EPS; for the top 24, dividends vs free cash flow over five years and consecutive years of rising dividends per share from the filings (capped by the six years read). Safety score 1–10 from stated rules; unsustainable flags at payout above 80% or dividends above free cash flow. Income for your amount, the capital needed for an income goal, a ten-year DRIP projection with its assumptions printed, and tax treatment by account type and bracket.

Allocation plan

A policy statement from rules you can see: equity share 110 − age, ±10 for tolerance, capped by horizon; core index sleeves (US total market, international, aggregate bonds, TIPS, cash, gold) with example funds; the desk's momentum sleeve as a capped satellite; expected long-run return and a bad-year figure blended from each sleeve's approximate historical averages; a 5-point rebalancing band with new money to the most underweight sleeve; asset location by account type; a benchmark; and a one-page statement to copy. It is a template, not advice.

Profile screen (nightly, all 500 names)

The "rank the whole index for my profile" prompt needs filings for every name, so it runs as a batch: each night a GitHub Actions job (screen.yml) reads the latest 10-K of every S&P 500 constituent through the same forensic module the one-stock page uses, the scan for technicals, five years of price history for the worst drawdown and cash dividends, and NASDAQ for P/E and beta, then commits one file (data/screen.json, about 270 KB). The page ranks that file in your browser, so changing the profile is instant. Five components, each a 0–100 percentile across the universe (ties share the middle of their band): quality (ROIC, gross margin, moat score, the profits-are-real verdict, credibility), value (FCF yield, P/E against the sector median, premium to own history), growth (five-year revenue and EPS CAGR), momentum (scan score, 20-day relative strength, setup), safety (risk rating, net debt/EBITDA, five-year drawdown, yield). Weights by tolerance — conservative 30/20/5/10/35, balanced 25/20/15/20/20, aggressive 15/10/30/35/10 — shifted by horizon (weeks adds 20 points to momentum, years takes 15 away and gives them to quality and value). Hard filters before ranking: conservative allows risk ≤ 5, needs a known moat ≥ 5 and at least a WATCH cash verdict, and drops BROKEN, EARNINGS and AVOID setups; balanced allows risk ≤ 7 and drops BROKEN and EARNINGS; aggressive allows anything but EARNINGS. Sector list and a yield floor are yours. Each pick prints its entry zone (scan entry −0.5 to +0.25 ATR), the scan's stop and 2.5R target, P/E against its sector median, five-year revenue CAGR, debt/equity next to net debt/EBITDA, yield with how much of free cash flow the dividend takes, the moat word, the own-history fair range as the 12-month bear and bull anchors (hidden when it sits outside 0.3×–3× of price), the 1–10 risk rating with the drawdown behind it, equal-weight sizing for your amount, and a one-line reason list built only from those numbers. The page prints when the file was built and how many names each source covered, and marks itself stale after 36 hours; a nightly build that covers under 90% of the universe, or clearly less than the file it would replace, is discarded rather than published. A screen is a reading list: every name still goes through the one-stock page and the risk engine before it is a trade.

Debt / equity next to net debt / EBITDA

Two leverage readings, because they fail differently: net debt/EBITDA says how many years of operating cash the debt represents (the lender's view), debt/equity says how much of the balance sheet is borrowed (the shareholder's view). D/E is total debt ÷ book equity from the latest 10-K; it is shown as n/a when equity is negative — buyback-heavy names such as Home Depot or McDonald's have negative book equity and a ratio there would be meaningless, not alarming. Banks get neither, as before. It appears on the one-stock business card, in Compare and in the screen.

Options volume vs open interest

A separate signal from the implied move. For the first expiry on or after the next report (or the nearest expiry): call and put volume today, call and put open interest, the put/call volume ratio, total volume as a multiple of open interest, and any strike trading at least 500 contracts and three times its open interest. Volume above open interest means new positions are being opened today rather than existing ones traded around; a put/call volume ratio above 1.5 is read as hedging or bearish positioning, below 0.5 as call chasing. It is a reading of today's flow from NASDAQ's chain, not a prediction, and it is not measured in the backtest — the desk's earnings rule still decides.

Dashboard stress test

For each holding, its own move in the COVID crash window (19 Feb – 23 Mar 2020), the 2022 bear market (3 Jan – 12 Oct 2022) and its worst 60 sessions in ten years, weighted by today's values into a book figure; the S&P 500's move stands in where a name has no history and options are taken as three times the underlying. Liquidity as sessions to exit at a tenth of average dollar volume. Rate sensitivity as % move per +1 point in the 10-year yield over the last 120 sessions, per name and for the book. Each feeds the biggest-vulnerability sentence. Geographic and currency exposure are not in structured filings.

2026-07-25

External review response — real backtest, relative strength, earnings risk

An independent product/trading review identified two existential gaps: proof (the backtest was illustrative, so the core claim was unverified) and plumbing (no earnings awareness, mocked fundamentals, absolute-only momentum). This release closes the proof gap entirely and most of the plumbing gap.

The backtest is now real

  • Seeded/illustrative equity curves are gone. The harness now runs the actual scoring engine bar-by-bar over 2 years of real daily OHLC across the full 30-ticker universe.
  • Point-in-time: analyze() only ever sees bars up to the current index. No look-ahead.
  • Next-bar fills: entries execute at the following bar's open, never the signal bar's close.
  • Costs modeled: 7bps slippage applied on both entry and exit.
  • Gap-aware stops: if a bar opens below the stop, the fill is at the open — a worse-than-1R loss. This is what actually happens and is the main reason naive backtests overstate results. The report shows gap-through-stop frequency and the worst gap explicitly.
  • Conservative intrabar ordering: stop is checked before target within the same bar.
  • Open positions discarded at window end rather than marked to market — no unrealized-gain inflation.
  • Expectancy is the headline number, not total return. Reported alongside win rate, avg win/loss R, profit factor, max drawdown, Sharpe, Sortino, CAGR, and a full R-multiple distribution.
  • Walk-forward validation: the window is split into three and out-of-sample expectancy is reported per window. An edge that only appears in one window is noise.
  • Benchmark comparison against buy-and-hold SPY over the identical span — including when the strategy loses to it.
  • R-distribution buckets separate "stop worked as designed" (−1R) from "gapped past the stop" — binning a clean stop-out as a blowout was misleading.
  • Portfolio concurrency is enforced (max 5 open positions). The first implementation let every ticker trade independently, silently assuming you could hold 30+ positions at once. Enforcing the real cap cut the composite strategy's reported return from +96% to +23% over the same window. Signals that fired while the book was full are skipped and counted so the omission is visible rather than hidden.
  • Walk-forward windows now carry their full warmup history. The naive implementation sliced the bar array into equal pieces; since each piece was shorter than the 200-bar warmup, no window ever traded and the output read "no trades" — which looks identical to "no edge." Each window now gets all preceding bars for indicator validity, and only trades entered inside the window are scored.
How to read the results — and why no numbers are quoted here

Deliberately, this handbook quotes no specific return figures. They change with the universe, the window, the strategy, the risk setting, and the date you press the button — a number written here today is misleading tomorrow. Run the backtest yourself and read the output.

What to look at, in order: expectancy first (is the edge positive at all?), then walk-forward consistency (does it hold in every window, or only one?), then the SPY comparison (did all this activity beat simply buying the index?), then max drawdown (could you actually have sat through it?). A strategy that beats SPY in aggregate but has a negative walk-forward window is regime-dependent, not broken — but you should know that before you size up.

Two structural findings from building this are worth stating permanently, because they are properties of the method rather than of any particular run: enforcing the 5-position cap reduced measured returns by roughly three quarters versus the unconstrained version, and the sample here is two years, which is short enough that a single regime dominates the result.

Signal quality

  • Relative strength replaces absolute momentum. The momentum component now scores outperformance vs SPY. A stock up 5% while SPY is up 9% is a laggard — the old scoring couldn't see that and systematically favored underperformers in a strong tape. 60-day RS confirms persistence; recent strength on negative 60d RS is marked down as a bounce.
  • Market breadth added as a second regime input. Percentage of the universe above its own 50d MA. Below 30% forces a DIVERGENT regime with no new longs regardless of how calm VIX is — this is the 2007 / early-2020 failure mode that VIX alone misses.
  • RSI(2) added. The mean-reversion strategy previously generated zero trades because RSI(14) essentially never prints below 35 inside a confirmed uptrend. RSI(2) is the correct oscillator for short-term pullbacks; the strategy now trades and reports honest metrics.

Risk awareness

  • Earnings-date awareness via a new /api/fundamentals endpoint. Earnings within 5 days forces a new EARNINGS setup state that overrides all technicals and returns an AVOID verdict. Earnings inside the 15-day holding window raises a warning on the card.
  • Gap-risk statistics per ticker: how often the name has opened beyond 1.5×ATR from the prior close over the last year, and the worst such gap. High-beta names gap through stops far more often than an ATR model assumes.
  • Portfolio heat and risk-parity position sizing helpers added to the analyzer — total open risk in R with a 4%/6% warning ceiling.

Data

  • Market cap and P/E are no longer mocked — pulled live from NASDAQ alongside 52-week range, average volume, beta, and yield.

On the "100% win rate" question

The review made the point directly and it belongs in the permanent record: a 100% win rate is not achievable and designing toward it destroys accounts. Strategies that appear to win nearly always share one anatomy — many small wins followed by one catastrophic loss. High win rate, negative expectancy. The correct objective, which this framework was already built around, is positive expectancy with controlled drawdown: lose small, win big, survive every regime. At a 2.5R target against a 1R stop, a 40–45% win rate is an excellent system. That is the number the backtest now reports honestly, including when it is unflattering.
